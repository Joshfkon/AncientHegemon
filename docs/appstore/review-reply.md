Hello,

Thank you for reviewing Ancient Hegemon. I believe the app meets guidelines 4.3(a), 4.3(b) and 4.2.6. It is an original game, adapted from a board game I designed myself, and it was not built from a template or by an app generation service. My answers to your questions follow.

**1. What the app does and the problem it solves**

Ancient Hegemon is a single-player, turn-based strategy game set at the end of the Bronze Age (about 1200 BC). It is adapted from my own tabletop board game of the same name. The player leads one of four empires (Greece, Egypt, Italia or Mesopotamia) against computer-controlled rivals.

What sets it apart is that the hard part isn't winning battles. It's keeping an empire fed, paid, supplied and loyal:

- Every army eats grain, and armies cut off from home starve.
- Bronze needs both copper and tin, which must be found, traded for or conquered.
- Large standing armies drain the treasury and stop the population from growing.
- Newly conquered land pays little and is restless.

It also plays out across a living world:

- Each map is generated fresh every game, or the player can choose the classic Mediterranean board.
- Every land outside the empires belongs to a kingdom with its own ruler, temperament and memory. The player can trade with these kingdoms, win them with gifts, cow them into vassalage (they may refuse, shirk levies or rebel later), or conquer them.
- Rival rulers are met face to face in their throne rooms to negotiate marriages, pacts, alliances, trades, embargoes and tribute.
- Battles are fought on a 5×5 battlefield where the player chooses a formation and battle plan.
- Rulers age and die, and succession crises can split an empire.

The problem it solves: deep historical strategy games are mostly built for PCs and long sessions. Mobile strategy games are mostly either simple conquest games, where territory is everything, or free-to-play games built around timers and purchases. Ancient Hegemon gives players a complete strategy game on iPhone that can be played in short turns, with no account, no ads, no network connection and no timers.

**2. The intended user**

- Adults and older teens who enjoy turn-based strategy and history.
- People who play or have played historical board games and computer strategy games, and want one they can play on their phone in short sessions, offline.
- Players new to the genre: an optional guided opening slows the first rounds while in-game advisors explain taxes, armies, kingdoms and diplomacy.

**3. The need or gap it addresses**

The most popular strategy games on the App Store reduce strategy to taking territory. Ancient Hegemon is built on the opposite idea, that territory alone does not make a power:

- Supply lines, bronze-age resources (tin and copper) and the cost of keeping armies decide what a realm can do.
- A small, well-built realm with good trade can win, and the game is balanced and tested so it can.
- The independent kingdoms act on their own, remember how they were treated, and can be traded with, allied, made vassals, or conquered. They are not passive territory waiting to be painted.
- Diplomacy happens in person with each ruler, not through a menu.
- It is set in a specific, rarely covered period, the Late Bronze Age collapse. Late in each game the world itself turns hostile: raiders, drought, earthquakes, and trade routes failing.

It is also a complete game with nothing to buy: no advertising, no in-app purchases in this version, no data collection and no internet requirement.

**4. Beta testing, and feedback applied to the production build**

I tested the game myself through TestFlight across many builds, playing complete games on my iPhone after each round of changes and feeding what I found back into the next build. I also invited a few other people as testers. Changes that came from that play-testing include:

- **Feedback that the game started too abruptly.** I added an optional guided opening with quiet first rounds, in which the player is not attacked and advisors introduce each system as it becomes relevant.
- **A crash when tapping "Show me" on an advisor's message, which also showed a raw internal name.** Fixed in the next build.
- **"It's hard to deselect an army; tapping a country tries to march there."** Picking an army and looking at a land are now separate actions.
- **"The first optimal moves are the same every game."** I added a map generator, so every game is played on a new world.
- **"I can make any small region a vassal immediately and use its troops to take everything."** Vassalage now depends on each people's temperament and is not guaranteed. Vassals may refuse levies, and they may rebel when the overlord is weak or a new ruler takes the throne.
- **"The only way to get my neighbour's timber is to conquer them."** I added trade agreements with the kingdoms, priced by distance.
- **"It's too easy to turn your whole population into troops; it feels like Risk, where territory is everything."** Standing armies now cost upkeep and limit growth, defenders raise levies from their population, and buildings and trade give prestige. Expanding and building are now a real trade-off.
- **"There's no way to disband fleets."** Added.

**6. Could this be delivered inside another app on the account?**

No. Ancient Hegemon is a complete game in its own right, with its own world, rules and content; it is not an expansion or variant of any other app on my account.

**7. Shared code or assets with other apps on the account?**

No. Ancient Hegemon has its own codebase:
- a dedicated rules engine (map, economy, battles, AI and diplomacy) written for this game;
- its own interface.

All of its art (the map, rulers, throne rooms, cities and battle pieces) is drawn in code for this game. Its music and sound effects were composed and generated by scripts written for this game. It shares no code, frameworks or assets with my other apps.

**8. Shared codebase, SDK or content library with a third-party app?**

No. The app uses only Apple's own frameworks (SwiftUI and AVFoundation). It has no third-party SDKs, engines, templates or content libraries. The game design, rules, map, historical content, art, music and text are original to this project.

**9. Created for a client or third party, or from a template or generation service?**

No. Ancient Hegemon is my own game, adapted from my own board game, published under my own developer account. I designed the board game years ago and developed this iPhone adaptation this year. It was not made for a client, and no template, app builder or app generation service was used. I wrote the game with the help of an AI coding assistant (Claude Code), working in my own source repository under my direction. The design, rules and content are mine.
